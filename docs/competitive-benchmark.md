# TrialIQ competitive product direction — August 2026

TrialIQ should learn from the strongest U.S. clinical/life-sciences platforms without trying to reproduce their scale.

## Benchmark signals

- **Medidata:** unified clinical architecture, validated historical clinical data, proactive risk detection and next-best actions. TrialIQ implication: intelligence must be contextual, traceable and action-oriented rather than a generic summarizer.
- **Veeva:** risk-based data management, automated orchestration, smart automation and innate traceability. TrialIQ implication: use deterministic rules where precision is required and AI where contextual reasoning adds value.
- **Saama:** AI-assisted clinical data review and discrepancy/query automation. TrialIQ implication: reduce reviewer effort by detecting issues continuously and preparing review-ready actions.
- **ConcertAI:** agentic workflows spanning protocol design, feasibility, site selection, matching and monitoring. TrialIQ implication: connect signals across modules and eventually turn findings into workflow actions.
- **Medable:** protocol-to-structured-data and agentic clinical-development workflows. TrialIQ implication: protocols should become machine-readable requirements, not just documents.
- **Tempus:** longitudinal multimodal data, agentic drug-development workflows and patient matching. TrialIQ implication: preserve longitudinal context and distinguish new change from known findings.
- **TriNetX:** large-scale RWD, standardized data, provenance and decision-grade analytics. TrialIQ implication: provenance and evidence lineage are product capabilities, not metadata afterthoughts.
- **Flatiron:** high-fidelity clinical/RWD with strong clinical context. TrialIQ implication: prioritize evidence quality and contextual interpretation over model novelty.

## TrialIQ differentiation

TrialIQ should be the **clinical decision intelligence layer** that turns fragmented evidence into a traceable review path:

`Evidence → Deterministic checks → Evidence graph → Reasoning → Decision readiness → Missing evidence → Next action → Human review → Clinical memory`

The product should answer five questions for every material issue:

1. **What is observed?**
2. **Why does it matter?**
3. **What evidence supports it?**
4. **What could change the conclusion?**
5. **What should the reviewer do next?**

## Product guardrails

- Never convert temporal association into causality without supplied support.
- Never infer a negative criterion from missing documentation.
- Never treat a single participant observation as a population signal.
- Keep deterministic protocol comparisons separate from probabilistic AI interpretation.
- Separate finding confidence, evidence completeness and decision readiness.
- Preserve source IDs and relationship evidence for every material conclusion.
- Optimize for risk-based review: focus human attention where a decision can change.
